# who is gdpr actually for

if you've ever actually tried to be gdpr-compliant at a small startup you know the drill. cookie banners that aren't dark patterns. a dpa with every vendor. sub-processor lists you keep up to date. a ropa nobody is ever going to read. the dpo question that nobody can give you a clean answer on. weeks of founder time eaten by a regulation you mostly agree with in principle. it's a pain in the ass, and you do it because you want to do the right thing.

meanwhile, the operators who don't actually care... just don't. consent banners nudging everyone toward "accept all," sub-processors that are basically a black box, data routed wherever the cheapest pipe goes. (and good luck finding the legal entity if you ever wanted to actually complain.) worst case, a fine in five years. probably nothing.

so who is this regime actually for? the well-intentioned eat the cost. the bad actors route around it. maybe gdpr's real job is to set a baseline norm, or give regulators a stick for the obviously egregious cases, or just to give brussels something to ship. i don't really know. it just feels strange that the tax falls hardest on the people who were already going to behave, and barely lands on the people it was supposedly written for. anyway. probably worth thinking about more than i have. idk.
