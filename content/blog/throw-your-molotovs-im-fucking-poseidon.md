I've been to a few meetups recently with early-stage founders.

Small groups. Founders among founders. People talking openly about where they are, what's working, what's not, and what keeps them awake at night.

And I've noticed something that feels very different from my own path as an entrepreneur.

There's a lot of doubt.

I actually admire the openness. Building a company is lonely enough without pretending everything is going great. Being able to sit among peers and say *I don't know* is healthy.

You should doubt things.

Doubt whether you're building the right product. Doubt whether you're talking to the right customers. Doubt your pricing. Doubt your assumptions. Doubt whether the thing you spent the last six months building is actually what the market needs.

But there's one kind of doubt I keep hearing that I think is dangerous.

Doubt about competitors.

*They're growing so fast.*

*They raised so much money.*

*They have all these customers.*

*How are we ever going to compete with them?*

Fuck that.

## I am fucking Poseidon

There's a particular mindset I think you need when you're competing.

Someone can stand on the shore and throw Molotov cocktails into the ocean.

Fine.

I'm fucking Poseidon.

Throw another one.

This isn't about pretending your competitors are idiots. They probably aren't. Some of them will be exceptionally good. Some will have more money than you. Better connections. A stronger brand. Fifty engineers while you have five.

Respect them.

Learn from them.

Steal every good idea you can legally steal.

But don't be afraid of them.

Because respect and fear are two very different things.

The moment your internal conversation becomes *How can we possibly compete with these guys?*, you've already given them something they didn't earn.

Your conviction.

And you're going to need every bit of it.

## The road to the throne is too fucking hard

Building a startup is hard.

Not LinkedIn hard.

Actually fucking hard.

For years, you're walking toward a castle that you can barely see.

Somewhere inside that castle is a throne. Maybe it's becoming the category leader. Maybe it's building a profitable company. Maybe it's the acquisition. Maybe it's simply reaching the point where the thing no one believed in becomes undeniably real.

Whatever your throne is, the road there is long.

And you're not walking it alone.

Everyone wants into the castle.

At first, you think you'll walk through the front gate.

Then you get closer.

The gate is closed.

You knock.

Nothing.

You knock harder.

Still nothing.

And eventually you realize: nobody is going to open the fucking gate for you.

So what now?

You find the back door.

And if the back door is locked, you break the latch off.

That's entrepreneurship.

Again and again and again.

The obvious distribution channel doesn't work. Find another one.

The customers you expected don't care. Find the ones who do.

The incumbent owns the enterprise market. Go underneath them.

The competitor raised €50 million. Good for them. Find the place where €50 million makes them slow.

There is always another door.

Your job is to keep looking until you find it.

## This doesn't mean you think you're invincible

You aren't.

You might lose.

Your competitor might genuinely have a better product. You might run out of money. You might make the wrong strategic call. The market might disappear underneath you.

Conviction isn't certainty about the outcome.

It's certainty about how you're going to show up.

It's looking at a competitor who is objectively ahead of you and thinking:

*They're fucking good.*

And then:

*How do we beat them?*

Not *Can we beat them?*

How.

That's a very different question.

One produces anxiety.

The other produces action.

## Maybe this doesn't come naturally to you

That's fine.

For some founders, this attitude seems almost pathological. They genuinely believe they'll win.

For others, doubt comes much more naturally.

If that's you, I think you need to consciously train the opposite response.

When you see a competitor announce something impressive, notice what happens in your head.

If your first thought is *Fuck, we're falling behind*, don't stay there.

Force the next question.

*Okay. What are we going to do about it?*

You don't need to become an asshole.

You don't need to publicly shit on competitors.

You don't need to walk around telling everyone you're better than them.

This isn't about them.

It's about what happens inside your own head.

Because the road to the throne is too fucking hard to spend it wondering whether the person walking next to you deserves to be there more than you do.

Respect them.

Watch them.

Learn from them.

Then keep walking.

And if they want to throw Molotov cocktails?

Let them.

You're fucking Poseidon.
