# You can't automate what you can't picture

I haven't written much this month. Bit busy, and honestly a chunk of that "busy" was running. I'm back at it properly now, and the injury is more or less under control (finally). Touch wood.

The other thing eating my time has been a run of conversations with people about automating the boring parts of their work with skills and agents. And I note sometimes hitting the same wall.

Explaining the tech is the easy bit. Getting someone to picture it is where it falls apart. You can tell someone the agent loop is powerful, that it can read, decide, act, check its own work, and loop until the thing is done, and they'll nod along. But ask them what in their own week they'd actually hand to it and they go blank. The leap from "this is powerful" to "this specific annoying task I do every Tuesday could just... not be mine anymore" is surprisingly big.

And I get why. It takes two things at once. You need a real feel for what the agent loop can do, and you need the creativity to look at your own work and go "huh, that's a process, an agent could run that." Most people have neither. It's not a smarts thing. Nobody's ever shown them what an automatable task looks like, so of course they can't spot one in the wild.

So I built a thing to help. It's a little catalog of skill and automation ideas, browsable at [stuff.barts.space/skill-atlas](https://stuff.barts.space/skill-atlas). Poke around, see what kind of boring work other people are handing off, maybe something clicks for your own week.

But the part I actually care about is the prompt. There's a copy-paste prompt on there that turns the agent into your brainstorming partner. It asks about your work, the stuff that drains you, and helps you find the bits that are genuinely automatable. Instead of me sitting across a table from you dragging the ideas out, the agent does it. Which is sort of the whole point, isn't it.

Have a play with it. And if you automate something good, tell me, I collect these.

Cheers.
